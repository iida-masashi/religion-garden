| Title | Taiwa Kyōdan | 
| Text | A Shinto-derived new religious movement. It emerged from [Yamatokyō](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9125), a movement founded by Hozumi Kenkō (1913-76) and his wife Hisako (1908-2003), when the latter movement's Sendai branch, headed by Hisako, went independent. Hozumi Hisako had been subject to chronic illness since childhood, but had overcome her ills by performing religious practice under the guidance of [an](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9654) ascetic from the Ontake cult. In 1934 Hisako married Kenkō, who also aspired to religious practice, and together they undertook religious exercises within the movement [Jikkōkyō](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9818). In 1935 they set up the [Jikkōkyō](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9818) Yudonosan Shinshū branch church, then broke away from [Jikkōkyō](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9818) in 1946 to establish [an](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9654) independent group which they called [Yamatokyō](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9125). Hisako and Kenkō later separated, and in 1957 Hisako established the [Taiwa Kyōdan](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9761) with herself as leader, registering it as a religious corporation. It was claimed that various *[kami](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=8996)* and buddhas, including [Amenominakanushi](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=9429) ōkami, Ōmonononushi ōkami, and the bodhisattva Taiwa Jizō Bosatsu would descend into Hisako, and at the end of each year a festival was observed in which she revealed divine oracles for the coming year. Members also perform mountain ascents at Dewa Sanzan (the three sacred peaks of the Dewa region) and carry out various other forms of religious discipline. In addition, the movement engages various social issues, such as campaigns for world peace, the abolition of nuclear weapons, and for disarmament. Headquarters: Miyagi Prefecture Nominal membership: approximately 57,000 (M) —Yumiyama Tatsuya |
