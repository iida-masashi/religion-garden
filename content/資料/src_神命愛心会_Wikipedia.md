From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Japanese new religious movement

**Shinmei Aishinkai** (神命愛心会) is a Japanese [new religious movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_religious_movement "New religious movement") founded by Komatsu Shin'yō in 1976. Komatsu was born in [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama "Yokohama") in 1928; her mother was the successor to a hereditary line of [kannushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannushi "Kannushi") ([Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto "Shinto") priests). In 1976, an acquaintance of Komatsu's made a prophecy that a [kami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kami "Kami") was about to descend to Earth. Shortly thereafter, Komatsu was visited in turn by a [dragon god](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenlong "Shenlong"), [Kannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanyin "Guanyin") and [Amaterasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaterasu "Amaterasu"), the sun goddess. From that time forward, Komatsu dedicated herself to passing on knowledge from Amaterasu.

## Faith and practices

The Shinmei Aishinkai movement became an official religious organization in 1983. The focus of the movement's practices are [ritual purification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritual_purification "Ritual purification") (*okiyome* or *osame*) meant to give ascension to the spirits or *jaki* of departed people which are still bound to the earth. By doing so, the group hopes to ensure peace and prosperity throughout the world. To that end, rituals have been held following such events as the [fall of the Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall#The_Fall "Berlin Wall") and the [September 11 attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_attacks "September 11 attacks"). The movement's main festival, *Kamiyo Gyōretsu* "Whole World Procession", is held in [autumn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn "Autumn") at the main Tokyo shrine and includes a parade in which members dress in period clothing and proceed through the city.

The five main gods worshipped by followers are Amaterasu, [Takemikazuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takemikazuchi "Takemikazuchi"), [Ninigi-no-Mikoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninigi-no-Mikoto "Ninigi-no-Mikoto"), [Kannō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannō-ji "Kannō-ji"), and [Ame-no-tajikarao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ame-no-tajikarao "Ame-no-tajikarao"). However, others such as [Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto "Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto") are also regularly worshipped.

The Shinmei movement, like many Shinto-derived new religious movements includes a strong emphasis on divination and [Wu Xing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuxing_(Chinese_philosophy) "Wuxing (Chinese philosophy)") practices, known as *kigaku*. This includes the study of *kasō,* the floor plan of one's house and the influences of energy drawn from various compass directions and shares some similarities with [feng shui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feng_shui "Feng shui"). The group also has strong ties to [Ise Grand Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ise_Grand_Shrine "Ise Grand Shrine") ([Mie Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mie_Prefecture "Mie Prefecture")), where the goddess Amaterasu is believed to reside.

As of 2008, the group has approximately 50,000 members nationwide. Branch shrines have been established in [Hokkaido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaido "Hokkaido"), [Kyushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyushu "Kyushu"), Nasu ([Tochigi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochigi_Prefecture "Tochigi Prefecture")), and [Hyōgo Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyōgo_Prefecture "Hyōgo Prefecture"). The main shrine is located in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo").
