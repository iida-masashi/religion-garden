In the [Tenrikyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenrikyo "Tenrikyo") [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion "Religion"), the **Jiba** (Japanese: ぢば, 地場, 中心), also known as the **Ojiba** (おぢば), is the *[axis mundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_mundi "Axis mundi")* where adherents believe that [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Tenrikyo "God in Tenrikyo") created [humankind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humankind "Humankind"). The spot is located in the center of the main sanctuary at [Tenrikyo Church Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenrikyo_Church_Headquarters "Tenrikyo Church Headquarters"), located in [Tenri, Nara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri "Tenri"), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan "Japan") (coordinates: [34°36′04″N 135°50′36″E﻿ / ﻿34.601227°N 135.843227°E﻿ / 34.601227; 135.843227](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Jiba_(Tenrikyo)&params=34.601227_N_135.843227_E_)). It is marked by a sacred wooden pillar called the *[Kanrodai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanrodai "Kanrodai")* (甘露台).

In Japanese, using [kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji "Kanji"), *Jiba* can be written as [地場](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/地場 "wikt:地場"), and *Ojiba* as [御](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/御 "wikt:御")[地場](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/地場 "wikt:地場"). However, in Tenrikyo publications, it is typically written using only [hiragana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiragana "Hiragana").

## History

[Miki Nakayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miki_Nakayama "Miki Nakayama") originally identified the location of the Jiba in 1875 (May 26 according to the lunar calendar; June 29 according to the Gregorian calendar). As stated in this following except from Chapter 6 in *[The Life of Oyasama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_of_Oyasama "The Life of Oyasama")*:

> The day before, Oyasama had said to the people who had gathered at the Residence: "Since tomorrow is the twenty-sixth of the month, the Residence must be swept very clean." They obeyed Her orders and devoted special care to the sweeping.
>
> Oyasama Herself was the first to walk the cleaned ground. When She came to a certain spot, Her feet stuck fast to the ground. She was unable to move forward or to the side.
>
> She put a mark on the spot and then asked the others to walk blindfolded. Kokan, Nakata, Matsuo, Masu Tsuji, Yosuke of Ichieda Village, and others walked blindfolded, in turn. When they came to the same spot, they, too, stopped as if they were drawn to the spot and held.
>
> The exception was Masu Tsuji. Masu tried again, this time with her daughter Tomegiku on her back, and the result was the same as with the others. Her feet stopped at the spot.
>
> In this way, the Jiba, the place for the Kanrodai, was identified at about noon on June 29, which was May 26 by the lunar calendar, 1875.

## Significance

The significance of the *Jiba* is described in all three [Tenrikyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenrikyo "Tenrikyo") [scriptures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scriptures "Scriptures") – the *[Ofudesaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofudesaki "Ofudesaki")*, the *[Mikagura-uta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikagura-uta "Mikagura-uta")*, and the *[Osashizu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osashizu "Osashizu")*. Phrases in the scriptures define *Jiba* as simply "the origin," and more specifically "the origin where God began human beings," "the origin of this world," and "the origin of all things." In the context of Tenrikyo's [creation narrative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenrikyo_creation_narrative "Tenrikyo creation narrative"), the *Jiba* is said to be the spot where *[Izanagi-no-Mikoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izanagi-no-Mikoto "Izanagi-no-Mikoto")* and *[Izanami-no-Mikoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izanami-no-Mikoto "Izanami-no-Mikoto")*, the models of husband and wife, conceived the first children. The scriptures also assert that *Jiba* is the place where [God the Parent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Tenrikyo "God in Tenrikyo") and the [everliving Oyasama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakayama_Miki "Nakayama Miki") reside.

The *Jiba* is closely associated with Tenrikyo's understanding of [salvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvation "Salvation"). The core ritual of Tenrikyo's [liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgy "Liturgy"), the [Kagura Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_(Tenrikyo)#Kagura_Service "Service (Tenrikyo)"), is performed around the *Jiba*. The *Osashizu* and stories from *[Anecdotes of Oyasama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anecdotes_of_Oyasama "Anecdotes of Oyasama")* refer to Jiba as the place where one can request one's own salvation or the salvation of others, and accordingly an important religious practice for adherents is to make [pilgrimages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrimage "Pilgrimage") there.

## Pilgrimage

Pilgrimage to the Jiba is also known as "returning to the Ojiba (おぢば帰り, *ojiba-gaeri*). Originally, it was known as "visiting the parental hometown" (親里参り, *oyasato mairi*), but "visiting" was later changed to "returning" in order to emphasize the homecoming aspect of the pilgrimage. At the church headquarters, the greeting "welcome home" (お帰りなさい, *okaeri-nasai*) can be seen. The church headquarters also has dormitories for pilgrims called *tumesho* (詰所).

## Kanrodai

[![](images/250px-Tenrikyo_Kanrodai_sketch.jpg)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Tenrikyo_Kanrodai_sketch.jpg)

A sketch of the Inner Sanctuary of Tenrikyo Church Headquarters, which contains the *Kanrodai*



Main article: [Kanrodai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanrodai "Kanrodai")

The *[Kanrodai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanrodai "Kanrodai")* (甘露台) is a [hexagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagon "Hexagon") stand that marks the *Jiba*. Adherents believe that when the hearts of human beings have been adequately purified through the [Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_(Tenrikyo) "Service (Tenrikyo)"), a sweet dew would fall from the heavens onto a vessel placed on top of the stand.

## In Honbushin

In [Honbushin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honbushin "Honbushin"), a Tenrikyo-derived religion, the location of the kanrodai is known as the *kanrodai-no-ba* (甘露台の場).
