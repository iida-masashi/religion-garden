From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Former new Japanese religion



Not to be confused with [Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri_Kami_no_Kuchiake_Basho "Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho").

**Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho** ([Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): 天理神の打開場所) was a [Tenrikyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenrikyo "Tenrikyo")-based *[shinshūkyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_new_religions "Japanese new religions")* (Japanese new religion). Among the religious traditions derived from Tenrikyo, it is one of the pre-1951 [Religious Corporation Law](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/宗教法人法 "ja:宗教法人法") traditions that were generally unknown to the Japanese public at that time. However, Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho gave influence to the creation of another Tenrikyo-influenced religious tradition called [Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri_Kami_no_Kuchiake_Basho "Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho") (天理神之口明場所), in which its legacy is more significant than the *original* Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho.

## Origin of name

The name, Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho, can be translated as *the place where the truth of the kami/god of Tenri (heavenly principle) is conveyed* from a verse in [Ofudesaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofudesaki "Ofudesaki"), with one of the phrases that bears the connotation of *truth-conveying* originally expressed as *uchi**w**ake* in [Hiragana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiragana "Hiragana"). The element, *Uchiake*, is a native Japanese reading and the original verb can reasonably be a dialectal or uncommon reading of a verb, *buchiakeru* (打ち開ける) in which it means *to speak frankly* or *to open one's heart* with the additional religious context of expressing the act of conveying to the receivers (adherents) with ideas or words. The Japanese particle, *no* ([の](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/の "wikt:の")), in the name can be replaced with the kanji, [之](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/之 "wikt:之"), without changing the pronunciation.

## Early history

It was founded in 1934 by the foundress, Watanabe Yoso (渡辺ヨソ), who had been a follower of [Tenri Sanrinkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri_Sanrinkō "Tenri Sanrinkō"). Her religious title was Masako Kanrodai (政子甘露台), in which it denoted the reverence of the status of her being a *human being who experienced divine revelation* (tenkeisha). Watanabe Yoso's own religious organization became active right before the closure and the eventual revival of Tenri Sanrinkō by Katsu Hisano (勝ヒサノ) that occurred in 1935.

## Lack of recognition

Not much about this religious organization is known possibly due to the very short duration of activity and possibly due to the sparse amount of documentation about its foundress, doctrine, and its history during its existence. Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho was a religious organization that was not mentioned in the late 1930s pertaining to the violation of the [Peace Preservation Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Preservation_Law "Peace Preservation Law"), as [Honmichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honmichi "Honmichi") (then called *Tenri Hondō*), [Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri_Kami_no_Kuchiake_Basho "Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho"), Tenri Sanrinkō, and Sanri Sanfukugen (三理三腹元) were the four prominent Tenrikyo-based organizations that faced negative consequences.

A book that slightly covers the background history of Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho was published in 2019. However, the book mainly discusses its other spin-off new religion, Tenri Kami no Uchiwake Basho (天理神之打分場所).

## Legacy

The following two religious organizations were founded by former Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho followers.

* [Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri_Kami_no_Kuchiake_Basho "Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho") (天理神之口明場所, 天理神之口開場所)
* Tenri Kami no Uchiwake Basho (天理神之打分場所)

Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho is the ancestral religious organization of the currently active religions [Ōkanmichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ōkanmichi "Ōkanmichi") (おうかんみち) and [Kanrodai Reiri Shidōkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanrodai_Reiri_Shidōkai "Kanrodai Reiri Shidōkai") (甘露台霊理斯道会), both of which are headquartered in [Tochigi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochigi_Prefecture "Tochigi Prefecture"), in addition to [Kanrodai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanrodai_(Japanese_New_Religion) "Kanrodai (Japanese New Religion)") that is based in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo").

Its sister sects, [Kami Ichijokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kami_Ichijokyo "Kami Ichijokyo") and [Sekai Shindokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekai_Shindokyo "Sekai Shindokyo") are considered to be the surviving successors of Tenri Sanrinkō that are active today.

## Notes

1. [↑](#cite_ref-1) See the difference between *Tenri Kami no **U**chiake Basho* and *Tenri Kami no **Ku**chiake Basho*.
2. [↑](#cite_ref-5) 天理神之打開場所 would be the resulted spelling, but it is uncommon.
3. [↑](#cite_ref-11) The reading convention is the original pronunciation of Uchiake, Uchiwake, according to a passage in the Ofudesaki, using the [nanori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanori "Nanori") reading convention of [分](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/分#Japanese "wikt:分"), wake (わけ). The foundress of Tenri Kami no Uchiwake Basho (天理神之打分場所) is or was a possible relative of, or another individual with connections to, Watanabe Yoso (渡辺ヨソ), Watanabe Hideko (渡邉秀子).
4. [↑](#cite_ref-14) Each of the [kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji "Kanji") component in 口明 and 口開, from 口明け or 口開け, which means the action of commencing from beginning in Japanese denotes a sense of origination; 明 and 開 are pronounced as /a/ in the native Japanese reading convention (kun'yomi). Overall, Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho figuratively means *place of origin of the god of Tenri [principle of heaven]*.
5. [↑](#cite_ref-18) Ōkanmichi is headquartered in [Nasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasu,_Tochigi "Nasu, Tochigi"), while Kanrodai Reiri Shidōkai is headquartered in [Ōtawara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ōtawara "Ōtawara").
6. [↑](#cite_ref-19) Kami Ichijokyo was called Kami Ichijō Uchiake Basho (神一条打明場所) for a short period of time.
