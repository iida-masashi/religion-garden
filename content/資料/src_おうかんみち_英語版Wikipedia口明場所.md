From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tenrikyo-based Japanese new religion



Not to be confused with [Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri_Kami_no_Uchiake_Basho "Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho").

**Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho** ([Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): 天理神之口明場所) was a [Tenrikyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenrikyo "Tenrikyo")-based *[shinshūkyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_new_religions "Japanese new religions")* (Japanese new religion) that became formally independent in 1937 under its founder, Yamada Umejirō (山田梅次郎). Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho is a Japanese phrase that means *place of origin of the god of Tenri [principle of heaven]* in English. It is commonly treated as one of handful of sects that became separated from Tenri Sanrinkō, such as [Kami Ichijokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kami_Ichijokyo "Kami Ichijokyo") and [Sekai Shindokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekai_Shindokyo "Sekai Shindokyo"), due to the very short duration of existence of the *intermediatory* sect, [Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri_Kami_no_Uchiake_Basho "Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho").

## History

Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho was established by the *human [kanrodai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanrodai "Kanrodai")*, Yamada Umejirō (山田梅次郎). He was born on 8 May 1878 in [Kanie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanie "Kanie")-chō [Ama District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ama_District "Ama District"), [Aichi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aichi_Prefecture "Aichi Prefecture"). Umejirō had a history of changing religious affiliations many times. Initially, he joined Tenrikyo in 1899 due to a health issue and eventually became a junior lecturer of the faith, although he joined [Honmichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honmichi "Honmichi") in 1925, the year when it was founded by [Ōnishi Aijirō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ōnishi_Aijirō "Ōnishi Aijirō"). However, his religious affiliations kept changing. Afterwards, Umejirō also received a divine revelation (tenkei) in 1931 for the first time. Umejirō eventually became a follower of [Tenri Sanrinkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri_Sanrinkō "Tenri Sanrinkō") in 1933 and then [Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri_Kami_no_Uchiake_Basho "Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho") in 1934.

On 11 January 1936, Umejirō received another divine revelation that he was the reincarnation of [Nakayama Zenbei](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/中山善兵衛 "ja:中山善兵衛"), the husband of [Nakayama Miki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakayama_Miki "Nakayama Miki"), the founder of Tenrikyo. This became his source of motivation to found his own separate religious sect. Umejirō performed rituals such as ōgi no sazuke (扇の伺い, *prayer offering of the fan*) and iki no sazuke (息の授け, *healing offering of the breath*) that are defunct in Tenrikyo in addition to an esoteric salvation ritual called kanromizu sazuke (甘露水授け, *offering the waters of divine nectar*) that were performed in [Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo "Tokyo"), [Nara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_(city) "Nara (city)"), and [Nagano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagano_(city) "Nagano (city)"); eventually founded his own religious organization within his [Nagoya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya "Nagoya") residence, Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho, on 23 December 1937. His title as the leader of Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho was Negabu Kanrodai (根株甘露台) or Umenoki Kanrodai (梅の木甘露台).

However, Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho did not remain in peace. Umejirō was arrested under the [Peace Preservation Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Preservation_Law "Peace Preservation Law") on 22 November 1938 and died on 8 November 1941. Before his death, Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho was officially dissolved in 1939.

## Legacy

Followers and children of Umejirō later founded their own independent religious sects based on his religious traditions before and after the end of the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War "Pacific War").

* [Hinomoto Shinseikō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinomoto_Shinseikō "Hinomoto Shinseikō") (日の本神誠講), founded by Okamoto Tsue (1899–1985), a follower
* [Ōkanmichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ōkanmichi "Ōkanmichi") (おうかんみち), founded by Egami Toshitane (1904–1971), a follower
* Tsukihi Sansei no Michi Machitake Honbu (月日三世の道真知岳本部), founded by Yamada Some, the second daughter
* Shinwa Kyōkai (神和教会), founded by Yamada Kinji, the second son

Ōkanmichi is largely treated as the successor religious group of Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho due to Egami Toshitane's close affiliation with Yamada Umejirō. A noticeable splinter religious group of Ōkanmichi is [Kanrodai Reiri Shidōkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanrodai_Reiri_Shidōkai "Kanrodai Reiri Shidōkai") (甘露台霊理斯道会).

"さて天理三輪講から独立した天理神之口明場所も十数もの天理教系教団の母体となった教団であり、ここからは創始者・山田梅次郎の子供や弟子たちがそれぞれ教団を興している。おうかんみち、月日三世の道真知岳本部、神和教会、日本神誠講といった教団などがそうで、「天理神之口明場所系教団群」ともいうべきものを形成している。" [However, Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho, which was split off from Tenri Sanrinkō, was the mother organization of around ten Tenrikyo-based organizations as well, Yamada Umejirō's children and disciples created new religious organizations such as Ōkanmichi, Tsukihi Sansei no Michimachitake Honbu, Shinwa Kyōkai, and Hinomoto Shinseikō, enough to be grouped as Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho-based religious organization groups.]
