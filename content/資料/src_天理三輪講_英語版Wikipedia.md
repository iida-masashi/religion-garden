From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Former Japanese new religion



**Tenri Sanrinkō** ([Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_language "Japanese language"): 天理三輪講) was a [Tenrikyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenrikyo "Tenrikyo")-based *[shinshūkyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_new_religions "Japanese new religions")* (Japanese new religion) that became formally independent in 1933 under its founder, Katsu Hisano (勝ヒサノ) who was a follower of [Honmichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honmichi "Honmichi"), then called *Tenri Hondō* at that time. Formerly based in [Osaka Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Prefecture "Osaka Prefecture"), it no longer exists today; however, some new religions claim or recognize their past history ultimately from it through their founders, such as [Kami Ichijōkyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kami_Ichijōkyō "Kami Ichijōkyō"), [Sekai Shindōkyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekai_Shindōkyō "Sekai Shindōkyō"), or even further to [Ōkanmichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ōkanmichi "Ōkanmichi") (おうかんみち) and Kanrodai Reiri Shidōkai (甘露台霊理斯道会; also known by the name of its main temple, Yamato Daijingū 皇和大親宮). Adherents of Tenri Sanrinkō followed the tradition of Honmichi by revering the leader as the *living [kanrodai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanrodai "Kanrodai")*. The *kō* (講) in Tenri Sanrinkō refers to confraternity in Japanese religious parlance.

Tenri Sanrinkō was unique among Tenrikyo-based splinter organizations for promoting its missionary activities towards Tenrikyo adherents.

## History

Tenri Sanrinkō was established by the founder, the *[kanrodai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanrodai "Kanrodai")* Katsu Hisano, in March 1933 in present-day western part of [Hirakata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirakata "Hirakata"), Osaka Prefecture with a goal to reunite Tenrikyo and Honmichi together; after her organization's dissolution in May 1935 by rejoining Tenrikyo to achieve her aforementioned goal, but met with failure and decided to reestablish her organization later on after leaving Tenrikyo. During this time, Japanese religious societies before the start of the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War "Pacific War") largely explored the balance and dynamics among Shinto, Buddhism, and Christianity, as shown in the example of [Three Religions Conference](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/三教会同 "ja:三教会同") conducted by the Home Ministry on 25 February 1912. In the founder's case, she created a new doctrine by uniting the teachings of the three major religious traditions in Japan (Shinto, Buddhism, and Christianity) of her time with the name, United Teaching of Three Faiths (三教合一, *Sankyō Gōitsu*). Another new religion called Shūyōdan Hōseikai (修養団捧誠会), founded by Idei Seitarō (1899–1983), is an instance of schism from Honmichi that occurred earlier than Tenri Sanrinkō. Ultimately, Katsu Hisano's religious organization perished due to the [Peace Preservation Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Preservation_Law "Peace Preservation Law") and the enforcement of [lèse-majesté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lèse-majesté "Lèse-majesté"). Although Katsu Hisano died in 1941, her protégée, Yonetani Kuni (米谷クニ), founded Kami Ichijōkyō in 1942.

## Legacy

Tenri Sanrinkō became a source of many other new religious organizations in Japan. As the [eschatological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eschatology "Eschatology") nature of Tenri Sanrinkō is also presented in its doctrine, its descendant religious organizations also contain teachings on eschatology as understood by Katsu Hisano. The following four religious organizations are founded by former Tenri Sanrinkō members.

* [Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri_Kami_no_Uchiake_Basho "Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho") (天理神の打開場所, 天理神之打開場所)
  + Tenri Kami no Uchiwake Basho (天理神之打分場所)
  + [Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenri_Kami_no_Kuchiake_Basho "Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho") (天理神之口明場所, 天理神之口開場所)
    - [Ōkanmichi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ōkanmichi "Ōkanmichi") (おうかんみち)
      * [Kanrodai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanrodai_(Japanese_New_Religion) "Kanrodai (Japanese New Religion)") (甘露台)
      * [Kanrodai Reiri Shidōkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanrodai_Reiri_Shidōkai "Kanrodai Reiri Shidōkai") (甘露台霊理斯道会)
* [Sanri Sanfukugen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanri_Sanfukugen "Sanri Sanfukugen") (三理三腹元)
* [Kami Ichijōkyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kami_Ichijōkyō "Kami Ichijōkyō") (神一条教)
* [Sekai Shindōkyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekai_Shindōkyō "Sekai Shindōkyō") (世界心道教)

Kami Ichijōkyō is a currently active religion whose founder claimed direct succession of Katsu Hisano's spiritual legacy through Tenri Sanrinkō.

Tenri Kami no Uchiake Basho was a sect that is today generally less studied within Japanese scholarly circles on religious studies. However, its related defunct organization called Tenri Kami no Kuchiake Basho left a bigger legacy as an intermediate entity to the creation of the currently active religions Ōkanmichi, Kanrodai Reiri Shidōkai, and others.
